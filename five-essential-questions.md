### 1. Central Theme & Argument

**Question:** What is the main thesis regarding how workplace AI usage creates severe career risks, and what core dynamic drives these threats?

**Answer:**
The main thesis is that **using AI without proper corporate governance or rigorous human verification is where careers go sideways**. Most career-ending AI mishaps are not committed by malicious actors, but by well-intentioned employees who believe they are simply being productive.

The underlying dynamic driving these risks stems from two key breakdowns:

- **Governance Breakdown:** When organizations lack a formal governance framework or clear usage policies, employees adopt unvetted tools, causing the organization to lose visibility into corporate data flows.

Ultimately, when a data breach or critical error occurs, **accountability falls squarely on the human employee who submitted the work or deployed the tool — not on the AI model**.

### 2. Key Supporting Ideas

**Question:** What are the five specific AI risk vectors identified in the source, and how does each function as a distinct operational or security threat?

**Answer:**
The source categorizes workplace AI dangers into **five distinct risk vectors**:

1. **Shadow AI (Unapproved AI Use):** The use of AI tools that corporate IT has neither vetted nor approved, such as using a personal ChatGPT account for work tasks or installing unapproved browser extensions on corporate laptops.
2. **Data Leakage:** Pasting sensitive proprietary code, customer records, or confidential content into unapproved third-party AI tools. Once transmitted to external servers, control is lost, and the data may be ingested to train future model iterations where it becomes permanently baked into model weights.
3. **Hallucination Laundering:** Taking plausible-sounding but completely fabricated AI outputs ("hallucinations") and copy-pasting them directly into work reports, presenting disposable "AI slop" as verified factual work backed by the employee's personal credibility.
4. **Prompt Injection:** Security vulnerabilities where an attacker crafts an input designed to override an AI system's original instructions or system prompt. This occurs via **direct prompt injection** (typing malicious instructions straight into a chatbot) or **indirect prompt injection** (hiding malicious instructions inside retrieved documents, emails, or web pages).
5. **Unauthorized Agentic AI:** Spinning up autonomous AI agents capable of reading/writing to databases, calling APIs, executing code, and sending messages without human oversight. A major threat is the **"zombie AI agent"** — an unmonitored agent left running after a project ends that retains active authentication and API keys, creating an unmonitored backdoor into enterprise systems.
